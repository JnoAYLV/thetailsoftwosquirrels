The legend

The war was won. The wound was not healed.

Long ago the squirrels fought a great war, and won it. But the victory tore a wound in the land, and the wound went grey and spread. Today it is the Dark Forest, and it creeps a little closer every year.

The old stories say that one day a squirrel will be born with a mark on its tail: an oak inside a diamond. That squirrel will lead, and will have the knowledge and the courage to heal the wound and bring back the light.

For seven generations the watchers of the Longwatch have checked the tail of every kit born on the Cradle Bough. Old Oswin has watched for fifty-one years and found nothing. Tonight, in the worst storm anyone can remember, he climbs up for the very last time.

Tonight, for the first time in fifty-one years, something on the Cradle Bough is about to change.

Oak in a diamond, born in the bough,
one day a heart, but nobody knows how.
Watch every tail, and keep your vow.

Who you'll meet

Lanterns, exiles, and things in the grey

  • The boy from the oak

    Conor

    Grows up in the kingdom at the heart of the great oak, where everyone knows his name. Clever, kind, and always planning. He can see six ways to do anything, and sometimes that is five too many.

  • The last watcher

    Oswin of the Longwatch

    Tells the best stories in the kingdom and comes to every birthday. A watcher all his life, his eyes go to your tail before your face.

  • The exile

    Hesper

    Walked away from the kingdom long ago and never came back. Quick with a needle, quicker with a reply. She hums a lullaby that always stops one line short.

  • The boy from the corner

    Cade

    Lives in the deepest corner of the Dark Forest, where nothing grows. He has never seen the sun. He once ran off three grey creatures with nothing but a pinecone, and he would like you to know that.

  • What the grey makes

    The Hollow

    Bark-grey, with knothole eyes and tails like dead branches. They take things and drop them and take again. Nobody knows what they want.

The world

A kingdom on one great tree, and a forest gone grey

The Arden Oak

The First Oak, older than the kingdom. Homes, bakeries and workshops wind up its trunk on spiral roads. High in its branches is the Market Ring, where moles deliver the post through hatches in the floor and the newest map is already out of date, because the grey edge keeps moving.

Kingsbark

The legendary armour in the Great Hall, made from bark the great oak shed. Its heart-plate has been missing for longer than anyone can remember.

Borrowed light

The Sapwardens draw a thread of light from living bark to fill the kingdom's lanterns. The Sapwardens' magic is borrowed, and it always costs the one who borrows it.

The grey edge

Where the colours stop. For generations the line between green and grey stayed put. Lately the sentries on the east wall keep finding one more grey tree than the day before.

The Dark Forest

A hundred shades of grey, and rot-glow on the trunks like chalk. Warm fog rises from the Wound, a great bowl nobody has seen the bottom of, and the dead leaves fall upward. Under one hole in the canopy lies a fallen statue no one remembers carving.

Next lantern on the trail: Chapter 6

Prologue · The Last Night of the Watch

The storm came up out of the Dark Forest the way storms always did, low and sideways, as if it had been crouching there all day and was glad to be let out.

Oswin of the Longwatch climbed into it.

He went up the Arden Oak one paw at a time, the way his grandfather had taught him: three claws in, test the bark, then move. Rain ran down the trunk in sheets. It got into his ears and under his coat and made the bark slick as river stone. Somewhere below, a shutter banged and banged.

He was old now. Old enough that his knees had opinions about every branch.

On his back he carried the lantern basket that every watcher in his family had carried before him: woven willow, darkened by seven lifetimes of paws, lined with soft wool.

In his coat pocket, wrapped in waxed leaf so the rain could not reach it, was a letter. He had written it four times. The fourth time, he had kept it short.

To the Council of the Kingdom. The Longwatch is ended.

Tomorrow, at sunrise, he would set it on the council table, and that would be that.

But tonight was still his. So he climbed.
